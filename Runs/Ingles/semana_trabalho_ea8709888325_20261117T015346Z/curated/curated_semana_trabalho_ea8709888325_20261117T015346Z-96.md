## Is there a 4-day week in France?

Not nationally. France's legal working week for full-time private-sector employees is 35 hours, and no law gives workers a 4-day week. The main trial is a voluntary experiment run by the organisation 4jours.work, with emlyon business school evaluating it. About twenty organisations took part in its first phase, in about thirty different formats. A second phase began in February 2026, and no results have been published.

Public employers have tested compressed weeks instead. In 2024 the government announced an experiment for State services in which agents could work four days while still completing 1,607 hours a year. Its first review was planned for summer 2025, and we found no published result. A union study of 18 local authorities reached the same conclusion: the four-day week there cannot come with a cut in working time. That is the same hours in fewer days, not a shorter week.

A Senate bill filed on 31 August 2026 would cut employer social contributions for firms that agree a collective week of at most 32 hours over four days. It is at first reading.

## Working in France

### Working hours

The legal working time for full-time private-sector employees is 35 hours a week, or 1,607 hours a year. Hours above it count as overtime, and a collective agreement may set a different weekly duration. A working day may not exceed 10 hours of effective work, and the week may not exceed 48 hours, or 44 hours on average over 12 consecutive weeks. Full-time employees usually work 38.8 hours a week (Eurostat, 2025).

### Paid leave and holidays

Employees earn 2.5 working days (jours ouvrables) of paid leave per month of work, which makes 30 jours ouvrables, or five weeks, a year. Counted Monday to Friday, that is 25 days.

### Remote work

In 2025, 31.4% of employees aged 15 to 64 worked from home usually (8.2%) or sometimes (23.2%), according to Eurostat.
