## Working in Spain

### Working hours

The legal maximum is 40 hours a week of effective work, averaged over the year, and 9 hours a day unless a collective agreement sets another distribution. At least 12 hours must separate one working day from the next (Workers' Statute, art. 34). Full-time employees usually work 39.2 hours a week (Eurostat, 2025). The employer must keep a daily record of each worker's start and finish times (art. 34.9).

### Paid leave and holidays

Annual paid leave may not be shorter than 30 calendar days (art. 38.1). Paid public holidays are capped at 14 a year, two of them local (art. 37.2).

### Part-time and flexible work

Workers may request adaptations to the duration and distribution of their working time (art. 34.8). Anyone with legal care of a child under 12 can reduce their daily hours, with a proportional cut in pay (art. 37.6).

### Remote work

In 2025, 12.8% of employees aged 15 to 64 worked from home usually (6.5%) or sometimes (6.3%), according to Eurostat.

- **Approval:** Employers can refuse based on operational needs
- **Scope:** Available to full-time employees in most sectors

A Belgian worker might request to work Monday-Thursday, 9am-7pm (with breaks), instead of Monday-Friday, 9am-5pm. They work the same 40 hours, just distributed differently.

**Benefits of Belgium's model:**

- Three-day weekends every week
- Reduced commuting costs (one fewer day)
- Better work-life balance for personal appointments
- Same salary and benefits

**Limitations:**

- Longer daily hours (10+ hours can be exhausting)
- Not a true reduction in working time
- Employer approval required (not guaranteed)
- May not suit all job types or industries

- Approval rates vary widely by industry
- More common in tech, professional services, and office work
