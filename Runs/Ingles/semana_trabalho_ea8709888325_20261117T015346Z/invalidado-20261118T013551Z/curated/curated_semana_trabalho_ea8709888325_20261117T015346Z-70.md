## What is a four-day week?

The four-day week model we work to is when people deliver 100% of their work, in around 85% of their contracted hours, for 100% of their pay. There is evidence that working a four-day week means people got more done in less time.

Some private sector businesses who work a four-day week choose to close for one whole day during the week - usually a Friday. This is not what the Council is doing. We maintain our full opening hours so that they are at least the same as they were before we started working in this way. You can continue to contact us on any day of the week that you choose.

Council officers are asked not to take on any additional paid work during their scheduled rest day. Colleagues use their scheduled rest day in a variety of ways, including caring responsibilities, volunteering, and to support their personal health and wellbeing.

### **Why a four-day week?**

The main reasons we trialed - and subsequently adopted - the four-day week include:

- To **help us attract and keep talented colleagues** in an incredibly competitive local employment market.
- So we can **improve our services by filling vacant posts permanently, rather than relying on more expensive agency staff.** Regularly changing staff, or using agency staff to cover posts, is both costly and disruptive to services for residents. For example, when case officers change during the process of a planning application, it can cause delays and frustration because a lot of context and institutional memory is lost.
- **To reduce costs.** During the four-day week, the Council has cut the amount it has to spend over its budgets on staff. There has been a yearly saving of £399,263. This is mainly due to filling many vacancies permanently – rather than using expensive agency workers, which can be disruptive for residents as officers change.

## Is there a 4-day week in United Kingdom?

No UK law gives workers a four-day week, and none provides a three-day one. In 2027 Christmas Day and Boxing Day fall on a weekend, so the substitute bank holidays are Monday 27 and Tuesday 28 December, leaving three regular weekdays that week. That is a calendar effect, not a change to the working week. Employees can ask to change their hours or days from day one, but have a right to request, not to receive.

Employers adopt it voluntarily. The 2022 pilot had 61 companies and around 2,900 workers on pay maintained at 100%; 56 continued. The 4 Day Week Foundation's 2024-25 pilot had 17 organisations and over 950 employees; all 17 were continuing (12 on a four-day week, five on a nine-day fortnight).

In the public sector, South Cambridgeshire District Council made a four-day week permanent in July 2025, though in October 2025 the Government wrote to its leader expressing 'deep disappointment' at a decline in performance in key housing-related services; the leader insisted it was 'an exceptionally high performing council'. In December 2025 the Communities Secretary reportedly told councils that part-time work for full-time pay without compelling justification would be one indicator, among a wide range of factors, of potential failure. A Scottish Government pilot put two public bodies on a 32-hour week with broadly stable performance, but the Scottish Government has ruled out a 32-hour week for itself and its agencies.

Caveat: pilot employers chose to take part, and most results are self-reported.

### **If we can be more productive on a four-day week schedule why can’t we be more productive but still work five days?**

Working a four-day week is very different from working a five-day week. It is more intense – it has to be, in order to complete 100% of the work in 80% of the time. Many people will have experiences of the end of their week being less productive than the beginning. In a four-day week, we expect the end of the week to be just as productive as the beginning – but we know that this level of intensity does mean the longer weekend is really required to recover and re-energise ready for the next week. Trials of a four-day week are going on elsewhere, and our trial tested this theory for us.

What we have also found with the four-day week is that employees have become more committed to the organisation, and their intention to stay with us has increased. This increases our productivity by reducing turnover costs – we can be more consistent in delivering services because we don’t waste time recruiting and training new colleagues. Before the four-day week this wasn’t the case – we had high turnover especially in some key service areas.

Some people think we should be able to be as productive over five days as we are over four. This is a bit like saying people running a marathon should run at the same speed as people running a sprint race – we know that’s not physically possible. It’s the same with a four-day week. The key for us is that all of the work is delivered, in 80% of the time. People feel more committed and so their motivation is higher. The office is open five days a week (and a little longer than usual on Wednesday evenings).

## Working in United Kingdom

### Working hours

UK workers average 36.3 hours a week (OECD, usual weekly hours of all employed people, 2025). In Scotland, public bodies covered by the Scottish Public Sector Pay Policy were strongly encouraged from October 2024 to move to a 35-hour week; all but one of 73 had done so or were expected to by April 2025.

### Paid leave and holidays

According to gov.uk, most workers on a 5-day week must receive at least 28 days' paid annual leave a year, the equivalent of 5.6 weeks. An employer can include bank holidays as part of that leave, and statutory entitlement is limited to 28 days. Bank holidays differ by nation: in 2027 England and Wales have 8, Scotland 9 and Northern Ireland 10.

### Part-time and flexible work

Employees can request a change to the number of hours they work, their start and finish times, the days they work or where they work, from their first day in a job. Section 9 of the Employment Rights Act 2025 adds a requirement that a refusal must also be reasonable and explained to the employee; it applies to England, Wales and Scotland and is only partly in force. Flexible working rules are different in Northern Ireland.
