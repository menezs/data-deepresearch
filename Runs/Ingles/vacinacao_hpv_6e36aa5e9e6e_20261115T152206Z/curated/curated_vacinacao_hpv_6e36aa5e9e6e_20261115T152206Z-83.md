## The ESCUDDO Study: Who Received Which Vaccine, and How Many Doses?

The study is a very large randomized clinical trial involving **20,330 girls aged 12–16 years** .

- Participants were randomized in a 1:1:1:1 ratio into **four arms** :
- **Bivalent HPV vaccine** – one dose
- **Bivalent HPV vaccine** – two doses
- **Nonavalent HPV vaccine** – one dose
- **Nonavalent HPV vaccine** – two doses
- For comparison, **3005 unvaccinated adolescent girls** were also enrolled in a separate observational survey group.

The main focus was infection with **HPV16 and HPV18** , the two most important HPV types responsible for cervical cancer:

**Primary endpoint:**

HPV infection arising between month 12 and month 60, persisting for at least **6 months** , involving HPV16 or HPV18.

To determine whether **one dose was noninferior—no worse than two doses by an acceptable margin—** the researchers set a threshold for the difference in infections: **It must be no worse than a difference of 1.25 infections per 100 people.**

## Is One Dose Worse Than Two? No.

**Noninferiority** analysis clearly shows that one dose **is not inferior** to two doses:

- **Bivalent vaccine:** One-dose minus two-dose difference**−0.13 infections / 100 people** (meaning there were actually*slightly fewer* infections in the one-dose arm)
95% CI: −0.45 to 0.15. The entire interval is well below the 1.25 threshold.
- **Nonavalent vaccine:** One-dose minus two-dose difference**+0.21 infections / 100 people**
95% CI: −0.09 to 0.51—also much better than the 1.25 threshold. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

The researchers can draw the following statistical conclusion: **With P < 0.001, one dose is noninferior to two doses for both vaccines**. In other words, there are no data to suggest that one dose is substantially worse; instead, protection levels are practically **the same**.

### Vaccine Efficacy: 97% and Above

The vaccinated groups were also compared with girls who remained unvaccinated during the same period. The result:

- In each of the four vaccine arms, **vaccine efficacy was at least 97%** .
- For HPV16/18 infections, this means a **risk reduction of more than 97%** for HPV infection compared with the unvaccinated group.

This does more than show that one dose is close to two doses; it also **clearly demonstrates that a single dose provides very strong protection in its own right** .

## Safety: Fewer Doses Do Not Add Side Effects

Throughout the study, **no serious safety concerns were reported**. In particular, regarding **severe (Grade ≥3) adverse events** :

- Rates of severe adverse events in the one-dose and two-dose arms were **similar** .
- There was also no clear safety difference between the vaccine types (bivalent and nonavalent).

In other words, **the single-dose strategy does not require an additional safety trade-off**. This offers a major advantage for public health programs: **fewer doses + similar protection + similar side-effect profile**.

# Is One Dose of HPV Vaccine Enough? What Does the ESCUDDO Study Published in NEJM Tell Us?

## In this article

The leading cause of cervical cancer is infection with human papillomavirus (**HPV**). HPV vaccines are among our most powerful tools for preventing this cancer and its related lesions. However, one of the biggest practical challenges is this: **Multidose vaccination schedules are difficult to implement, particularly in low- and middle-income countries.**

On December 3, 2025, a large clinical study published in **NEJM**(**ESCUDDO** Phase 3 trial) provided a crucial answer to this question: **One dose of HPV vaccine is no worse than two doses; it provides equally effective protection over time.** This finding could transform efforts to reduce HPV-related cancers worldwide.

**What does this article explain?**

We explain the results of the ESCUDDO study showing that one dose of HPV vaccine **is not inferior to two doses in protection** , including the numerical data and what they mean in practice.

**Why does it matter?**

Because **reducing the number of doses**can make vaccines more accessible to millions of girls and young people, reducing costs and logistical barriers and opening **a new chapter** in preventing HPV-related cancers.

## Why Are Fewer Doses of HPV Vaccine Being Discussed?

For many years, HPV vaccines have been administered in **schedules of 2 or 3 doses** , providing very strong protection. However, particularly in low- and middle-income countries:

- Difficulty returning for appointments,
- Transportation and organizational challenges,
- The cost of vaccination programs

mean that completion rates for these multidose schedules are below the desired level. Observational studies and smaller trials in recent years **suggested that even a single dose could provide very strong protection** . From 2022 onward, the World Health Organization also began accepting **schedules of 1 or 2 doses** for certain age groups. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

However, the **gold standard** in this field would be a clear answer from a large, randomized clinical trial. This is exactly what the ESCUDDO trial does: **Is one dose inferior to two doses or not?** It directly tests this question.

## Single-Dose HPV Vaccination: What Could It Change in the Fight Against Cervical Cancer?

HPV vaccination provides individual protection and, when widely used, can establish **herd immunity** , dramatically reducing cervical cancer incidence. Equivalent protection from one dose and two doses could be transformative in the following areas:

- **Vaccine access:** Single-dose schedules make vaccination programs**more feasible** , even in hard-to-reach areas.
- **Cost:** Fewer doses could reduce countries' vaccination costs and logistical burden**substantially reduce** .
- **Adherence:** The pressure of having to return for a second dose decreases, and even one appointment can provide**high protection** .

The World Health Organization aims to **eliminate HPV-related cervical cancer worldwide**. The apparent strength of single-dose protection is considered a development that could **shorten this process by years** . :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

## A Forward-Looking Perspective for Türkiye and Neighboring Countries

Among the most common topics in discussions about HPV vaccination in Türkiye and neighboring countries are **cost and program feasibility**. The ESCUDDO study tells us:

- Even a single-dose program could provide protection against HPV16/18, the main causes of cervical cancer, that is **extremely high protection** .
- For countries struggling to plan vaccination programs because of budget and logistical constraints, **a simpler, powerful option** is emerging.
- Evidence of this quality provides an important scientific foundation for **updating and strengthening** national immunization programs in the coming years.

Of course, each country's vaccination schedule is shaped by **local epidemiology, resources, existing programs, and health authorities' assessments** . However, the clinical message is clear: **Single-dose HPV vaccination has emerged as a strong and safe strategy, particularly against HPV16 and 18.**

## Conclusion: Fewer Doses, More Protection?

The ESCUDDO study sends a clear and powerful message: **One dose of HPV vaccine is not inferior to two doses in protection, and this conclusion rests on high-quality clinical evidence involving tens of thousands of young people.**

In the coming years, HPV vaccination schedules may be reshaped, with more countries considering single-dose strategies. This shift could be an important milestone toward **consigning cervical cancer and other HPV-related cancers to history** .
