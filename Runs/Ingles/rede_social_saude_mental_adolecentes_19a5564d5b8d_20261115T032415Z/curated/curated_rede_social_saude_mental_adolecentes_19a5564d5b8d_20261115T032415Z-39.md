The study provides some of the first findings about social media's possible consequences on the development of adolescent brains.

The study, published today in JAMA Pediatrics, reveals that adolescents’ brains may become more sensitive when anticipating social rewards and punishments over time with increased social media usage.

#### Longitudinal Whole-Brain Analyses

Missing data across waves were accounted for by using full information maximum likelihood, which provides an estimate of the value of a population parameter most likely to result in the observed data even in the presence of missing data.<sup>22</sup> We modeled a 3-way interaction with age (minimum centered), condition (reward and punishment anticipation), and social media checking to assess whether age-related changes in neural activation during social anticipation differed as a function of the type of social anticipation (ie, reward vs punishment) and amount of social media checking behaviors.
