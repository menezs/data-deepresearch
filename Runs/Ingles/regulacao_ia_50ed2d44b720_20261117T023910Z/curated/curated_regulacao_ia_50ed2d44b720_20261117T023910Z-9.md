## History and framework

The European Commission published its proposal for an AI regulation in 2021. The proposal suggested a risk‑based approach differentiating between 'unacceptable' risks, 'high‑risk', and 'limited risk'. The Council and the European Parliament made several changes; these included a chapter on general‑purpose AI (GPAI) models, which comprise large language models such as OpenAI's GPTs, Google's Gemini and Mistral's models. The final version of the regulation, the AI Act, was published in the Official Journal of the EU on 12 July 2024.

However, two years later, on 29 May 2026, the Council and the Parliament adopted Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, known as the Digital Omnibus on AI. This amends the AI Act to address implementation challenges, such as missing standards and the establishment of governance at national level.

#### Harmonised standards

The AI Act presumes high‑risk AI systems and GPAI models that conform to harmonised standards, published in the Official Journal of the EU, to be in compliance with the corresponding requirements and obligations.

The Commission requested that the European Committee for Standardisation (CEN) and the European Committee for Electrotechnical Standardisation (CENELEC) provide standards related to high‑risk AI systems in May 2023, with an initial deadline of April 2025. This deadline was missed. The request was updated in June 2025, with a deadline extended to August 2025, which was also missed.

The Digital Omnibus on AI explains that the delay in preparing the standards required is one of the reasons that the deadlines related to high‑risk AI systems were postponed. Since then, one harmonised standard related to quality management system for organisations that provide AI systems was published in October 2026. As of that date, the rest are still under development.

Regarding standards for GPAI models, as of October 2026, the Commission has not yet issued a request. The Commission instead points to the GPAI Code of Practice, which it presents as a practical compliance tool comparable to harmonised standards. However, only harmonised standards can provide the added benefit of a presumption of conformity.

## Implementation steps

The regulation's general date of application is 2 August 2026. However, several chapters, sections and articles have other dates of application and were changed by the Digital Omnibus on AI. As a result, the AI Act should be fully effective on 2 August 2028, when all rules for high‑risk AI will apply.

# AI Act – Implementation timeline

## Summary

The Artificial Intelligence (AI) Act regulates AI systems according to the risks they pose, and general‑purpose AI (GPAI) models according to their capabilities. Published in July 2024 and amended in 2026, the AI Act already applies in part and will take full effect in 2028.
