The FDA is requiring every glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA) to carry a new label warning about a potential increase in the risk of nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION), a rare but serious eye condition. The agency announced the requirement on November 9, 2026, directing all application holders to add the language to the Warnings and Precautions section of labeling.<sup>1</sup>

The requirement is classwide, covering exenatide, liraglutide, dulaglutide (Trulicity), lixisenatide (Adlyxin), semaglutide, tirzepatide, and the newly approved orforglipron (Foundayo), regardless of whether the product is marketed for diabetes or weight management.

NAION results from decreased blood flow to the optic disc, the front of the optic nerve, though the agency said the underlying cause is not completely understood. It typically presents as sudden, painless vision loss in 1 eye, often noticed upon waking, with blurred vision, blind spots, or color distortion. Vision loss is frequently permanent, and a prior episode raises the risk of a future one in the other eye.

#### **A safety signal against a widening neurology pipeline**

The warning lands as GLP-1 RAs are being tested across a growing list of neurologic diseases for their anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective potential. None of the trials below has reported a NAION signal, but each carries its own safety data that will now be read alongside FDA's warning.

In Alzheimer disease (AD), Novo Nordisk's phase 3 EVOKE and EVOKE+ trials tested oral semaglutide in 3808 adults with mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia due to AD. In Parkinson disease, 3 trials have tested different GLP-1 RAs with mixed results. A phase 2 trial of lixisenatide in 156 patients found a statistically significant slowing of motor disability progression at 12 months, but nausea occurred in 46% of treated patients vs 12% on placebo, and 1 patient developed treatment-related pancreatitis, prompting outside experts to caution that the drug was “not ready for prime time,” according to the investigators.<sup>4</sup>
