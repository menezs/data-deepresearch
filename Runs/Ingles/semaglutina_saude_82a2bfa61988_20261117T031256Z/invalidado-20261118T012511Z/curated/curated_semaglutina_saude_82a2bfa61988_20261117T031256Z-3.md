## A Hypothesis Built on Shared Biology

Much of the interest centers on glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs), the incretin-based class that includes liraglutide and semaglutide. In transgenic mouse models of AD, liraglutide improved memory and prevented synaptic loss while reducing β-amyloid and tau aggregation, neuroinflammation, and oxidative stress.<sup>1</sup> Investigators behind the semaglutide program framed their rationale around 2 early features of AD: glial activation and neurovascular dysfunction that can weaken the blood-brain barrier.<sup>2</sup>

Figure 4A-E depicts monthly changes from baseline with estimated effect sizes. Medication effect sizes were mostly small (by convention, Cohen _d_ = 0.20) through week 4 and increased during weeks 5 to 8, with large effect sizes (defined as _d_ = 0.80) observed for drinks per drinking day and heavy drinking days. Comparing the proportion of participants with zero heavy drinking days by group and treatment period 

(Figure 4F) showed that the likelihood of zero heavy drinking days increased significantly from weeks 1 through 4 to 5 through 8 in the semaglutide group ( _z_ score, −2.93; _P_ = .003), with no other significant comparisons.
