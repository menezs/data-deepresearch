### abstr act 

#### **BACKGROUND** 

Semaglutide, a glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist, is a candidate for the treatment of metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis (MASH). 

#### **METHODS** 

In this ongoing phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, we assigned 1197 patients with biopsy-defined MASH and fibrosis stage 2 or 3 in a 2:1 ratio to receive once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide at a dose of 2.4 mg or placebo for 240 weeks. The results of a planned interim analysis conducted at week 72 involving the first 800 patients are reported here (part 1). The primary end points for part 1 were the resolution of steatohepatitis without worsening of liver fibrosis and reduction in liver fibrosis without worsening of steatohepatitis.

Semaglutide, a glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist, is approved for the treatment of type 2 diabetes<sup>7</sup> and overweight or obesity,<sup>8</sup> with reports of improvements in cardiovascular and renal outcomes in these populations.<sup>9-11</sup> In a previous phase 2 trial, the effects of once-daily subcutaneous semaglutide (at doses of 0.1 mg, 0.2 mg, or 0.4 mg) as compared with placebo were investigated in patients with biopsy-defined MASH and liver fibrosis (stages 1, 2, and 3) during a 72-week period.<sup>12</sup> A significantly higher percentage of patients who received semaglutide at a dose of 0.4 mg than those who received placebo had resolution of steatohepatitis with no worsening of fibrosis (59% vs. 17%, P<0.001). In addition, patients in the semaglutide group had a reduction in fibrosis stage, although the improvement was not statistically significant. 

Currently, only resmetirom has accelerated approval from the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of MASH with stage 2 or 3 liver fibrosis,<sup>13,14</sup> with studies ongoing to establish the clinical benefits for full approval. Thus, there is an unmet need for additional treatment options. Moreover, given that patients with MASH are at high risk for adverse liver outcomes and cardiovascular disease, treatments should ideally address both conditions.<sup>15,16</sup> Here, we report interim efficacy results from the first 800 patients who completed 72 weeks of treatment in the ESSENCE trial evaluating once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide (at a dose of 2.4 mg) as compared with placebo in patients with biopsy-defined MASH and fibrosis stage 2 or 3.<sup>17</sup> Safety reporting is based on all data that were available at the time of the datacutoff date.

#### **RESULTS** 

Resolution of steatohepatitis without worsening of fibrosis occurred in 62.9% of the 534 patients in the semaglutide group and in 34.3% of the 266 patients in the placebo group (estimated difference, 28.7 percentage points; 95% confidence interval [CI], 21.1 to 36.2; P<0.001). A reduction in liver fibrosis without worsening of steatohepatitis was reported in 36.8% of the patients in the semaglutide group and in 22.4% of those in the placebo group (estimated difference, 14.4 percentage points; 95% CI, 7.5 to 21.3; P<0.001). Results for the three secondary outcomes that were included in the plan to adjust for multiple testing were as follows: combined resolution of steatohepatitis and reduction in liver fibrosis was reported in 32.7% of the patients in the semaglutide group and in 16.1% of those in the placebo group (estimated difference, 16.5 percentage points; 95% CI, 10.2 to 22.8; P<0.001). The mean change in body weight was −10.5% with semaglutide and −2.0% with placebo (estimated difference, −8.5 percentage points; 95% CI, −9.6 to −7.4; P<0.001). Mean changes in bodily pain scores did not differ significantly between the two groups. Gastrointestinal adverse events were more common in the semaglutide group.

#### **Trial Procedures and Outcomes** 

Patients were randomly assigned in a 2:1 ratio by means of a centralized system to receive onceweekly subcutaneous semaglutide (at a dose of 2.4 mg) or placebo in addition to standard care for MASH and related coexisting illnesses (Fig. S1 in the Supplementary Appendix). Lifestyle counseling and management of coexisting conditions were recommended in alignment with guidelines.<sup>19,20</sup> Randomization was stratified according to the presence or absence of type 2 diabetes at screening, fibrosis stage (2 or 3), and region (Japan, East Asia [excluding Japan], or the rest of the world). Semaglutide and placebo solutions were provided in prefilled pen injectors. 

The starting dose of semaglutide was 0.25 mg once weekly, which was increased every 4 weeks to once-weekly doses of 0.5 mg, 1.0 mg, 1.7 mg, and 2.4 mg until the target dose of 2.4 mg was reached after the 16-week dose-escalation period. Patients could continue receiving a lower dose if they had unacceptable side effects while receiving the designated target dose but were recommended to make at least one attempt to re-escalate to the designated target dose of 2.4 mg once weekly, per the investigator’s discretion. Additional details regarding the semaglutide dose selection are provided in the Supplementary Appendix. Semaglutide or placebo was discontinued in case of current or planned pregnancy, suspicion of acute pancreatitis, abnormal liver-enzyme values indicating drug-induced liver injury, or a diagnosis of medullary thyroid carcinoma.

#### **Statistical Analysis** 

The trial was designed to have 90% power to detect a 35% lower risk of a primary-end-point event in the semaglutide group than in the placebo group in part 2. In part 1, efficacy analyses were based on the intention-to-treat principle and included the initial 800 patients who had undergone randomization, regardless of their adherence to semaglutide or placebo or changes to concomitant medications. If a patient died or had a clinical liver end-point event — histologically confirmed cirrhosis, a MELD score of 15 or more, hepatic decompensation, or liver transplantation — before week 72, the patient was considered to have had no response. Missing data were handled by reference-based multiple imputation informed by observed placebo data. 

The absolute between-group difference in the percentage of patients with a response was calculated by means of the Mantel–Haenszel estimator<sup>21</sup> and Sato’s estimate of the standard error,<sup>22</sup> with stratification according to baseline diabetes status and fibrosis stage. Rubin’s rule was used to aggregate results and obtain 95% confidence intervals and P values. Stagewise ordering was used to adjust for the group sequential design.<sup>23</sup> 

If at least one of the two primary end points in part 1 had statistical significance, the primary part 1 objective of confirming benefits as compared with placebo in improving liver histologic features was considered to have been met. A graphical testing strategy was used<sup>24</sup> with a separate alpha-spending function<sup>25</sup> and the lookback approach<sup>26,27</sup> to preserve the overall onesided type I error at 2.5%. Collectively, this means that each primary end point was tested at a one-sided significance level of 0.00225 and a two-sided level of 0.0045. Additional details are provided in the statistical analysis plan, available with the protocol.
