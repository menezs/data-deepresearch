# NO FAKES Act advances unanimously out of Senate Judiciary Committee

On June 18, 2026, the Senate Judiciary Committee advanced the bipartisan NO FAKES Act by unanimous voice vote. The bill would create federal protections for unauthorized digital replicas of a person’s voice or visual likeness, but it is not law: a September 30 unanimous-consent request to pass it in the full Senate drew an objection.

The Senate Judiciary Committee advanced the NO FAKES Act unanimously by voice vote on June 18, 2026. The vote moved the bipartisan bill out of committee, but it did not enact the proposal or settle its eventual final text.

### What the committee advanced

The bill is named for the Nurture Originals, Foster Art, and Keep Entertainment Safe Act. Its sponsors say it would create federal protections against unauthorized digital replicas of a person’s voice or visual likeness in audiovisual works and sound recordings.

The Senate Judiciary Committee’s release says the proposal would make people or companies liable for distributing an unauthorized replica and would create potential platform liability when a platform knows that a replica is unauthorized. The release also describes recognized First Amendment exclusions and a preemption provision for future state laws regulating digital replicas. Those are proposed legislative terms, not current nationwide rules.

### What happened after committee

The bill reached the Senate calendar after the committee action. On September 30, Senators Marsha Blackburn and Chris Coons sought unanimous consent to take up and pass S. 4591. The Congressional Record shows that Senator Ted Cruz objected, so that request did not result in passage.

That later floor episode is important context for the committee vote: the bill had bipartisan committee support, but no final Senate passage is established by the retrieved record. It would still require the remaining legislative steps before it could become law.

### Why the stage matters

The proposal is material to services that generate, host or distribute synthetic voices and likenesses, but a committee vote is not a compliance deadline. Product and policy teams should distinguish the bill’s proposed liability and notice framework from existing law, and should not describe the NO FAKES Act as enacted based on the committee action alone.
