                                                        ABSTRACT: Trauma, bereavement and other adverse experiences can affect emotional wellbeing, relationships, education and everyday functioning. Responses vary, and self-reported exposure or symptoms should not be equated with a clinical diagnosis. This cross-sectional study explored reported experiences of trauma or bereavement, perceived disruption, recent trauma- or grief-related symptoms, coping, help-seeking, formal and informal support, and social-media effects among 78 respondents aged 13 - 28 years. The dataset was analysed descriptively using frequencies and percentages, with multiple-response items treated as overlapping categories. Exploratory Spearman rank correlations were calculated for selected ordinal-coded measures using pairwise complete observations. Fifty-two respondents (66.7%) answered yes to having experienced a traumatic event or bereavement in the previous five years; 20 (25.6%) answered no and six (7.7%) preferred not to say. Thirteen respondents (16.7%) were aged 13 - 17, while 65 (83.3%) were aged 18 - 28, limiting the extent to which the findings can be described as evidence about teenagers specifically. On the recent-symptom item, 42 respondents (53.8%) selected “Once or twice”; 21 (26.9%) selected weekly, several times a week or daily responses. Twenty-five respondents (32.1%) reported that formal support had been helpful, while 25 (32.1%) reported no formal support and 13 (16.7%) reported support that was not helpful. Social media was reported to make coping easier by 39 respondents (50.0%), have no effect by 20 (25.6%), have mixed effects by 17 (21.8%), and make coping worse by two (2.6%). The findings are descriptive and exploratory. The sample’s recruitment channels and geographic scope, complete questionnaire anchors, skip logic and ethics procedures of the original study were verified using the original study documentation. The paper concludes that accessible, confidential, developmentally appropriate and culturally responsive support merits further evaluation, without inferring causal pathways from cross-sectional data.

This umbrella review analyses the risks and opportunities for adolescents’ mental health and well-being associated with Social Media use and the main risk mitigation proposals presented in systematic, scoping and narrative literature reviews and meta-analyses. Following the PRIOR guidelines, we defined inclusion and exclusion criteria for Population, Exposure and Outcomes and searched articles published from January 2015 up to April 2023 in four databases: Scopus, Web of Science, PsychInfo, and Pubmed. We screened 1470 publications and analyzed 24  literature reviews and meta-analyses on the impact of SMU on adolescents' mental health. This paper discusses both the findings and the recommendations of the selected articles in terms of the main risks and opportunities associated with SMU, research and methodological gaps and practical risk mitigation measures. 
We discuss the limitations of not accounting for key differences both in the adolescent population and social media platforms and the need for the latter to adopt ethical design principles that take into consideration users’ best interests, arguing that it would contribute
to introducing a systemic change to achieve a population-level shift, which is harder to attain if the burden of responsible use is only attributed to individuals’ choices. Finally, we discuss the opportunities brought by the upcoming regulatory frameworks, such as the EU Digital Services Act, on the matter.
