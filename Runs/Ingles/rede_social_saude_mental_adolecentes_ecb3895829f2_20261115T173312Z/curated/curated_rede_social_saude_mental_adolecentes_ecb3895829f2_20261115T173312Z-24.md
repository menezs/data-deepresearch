### Summary 

This report provides important insights into the characteristics of females in the United States who had an SUD, an MDE, or both in the past year. The report also highlights how MDE and SUD pose significant public health concerns for females in the United States. Adolescent females aged 12 to17 and adult females aged 18 or older with a co-occurring SUD and MDE experienced significantly higher levels of suicidality than females in the same age groups with no past year MDE, those with a past year MDE but no SUD, or those with a past year SUD but no MDE. This elevated risk of suicidality aligns with the finding that most adolescent or adult females with both an SUD and an MDE in the past year experienced an MDE with severe impairment, indicating substantial disruption in daily functioning, relationships, and emotional well-being. 

Having an MDE with severe impairment in the past year also was common among females who had an MDE but not an SUD in that period. Roughly three fourths of adolescent females and nearly two thirds of adult females with an MDE but not an SUD had an MDE with severe impairment in the past year. This pattern shows that although having an SUD may increase the risk of suicidality, having an MDE without an SUD also can pose significant functional challenges for females. Similarly, having severe SUD symptoms was more common among females who had a co-occurring SUD and MDE than among females who had an SUD but not an MDE. Collectively, these findings indicate that adolescent and adult females with a co-occurring SUD and MDE experienced higher rates of MDE with severe impairment, suicidality, and more severe SUD symptoms. These patterns highlight the strong associations between co-occurring past year SUD and MDE and other symptoms associated with poorer mental health outcomes. 

However, estimates in this report are based on respondent self-reports and do not include people from certain populations that may have a higher prevalence of these conditions. Despite these limitations, the findings contribute valuable information on these conditions among females.

#### Suicidality in the Past Year: Among Females with an SUD/MDE in the Past Year; by Age Group, 2022-2024 

Co-occurring SUD and MDE in the past year among females was strongly associated with suicidal thoughts and behaviors (also referred to as suicidality). Among the annual average of 620,000 adolescent females aged 12 to 17 who had a co-occurring SUD and MDE in the past year, 59.1% also had serious thoughts of suicide, 39.7% made a suicide plan, and 25.9% attempted suicide in that period. These percentages are substantially higher than those among adolescent females who had only one of these conditions. For example, among adolescent females who had an SUD but not an MDE in the past year, 22.2% had serious thoughts of suicide, 12.5% made a suicide plan, and 10.6% attempted suicide. Among adolescent females who had an MDE but not an SUD in the past year, 42.9% had serious thoughts of suicide, 20.5% made a suicide plan, and 10.5% attempted suicide. 

A similar pattern was observed among adult females, with higher rates of suicidality among the annual average of 5.0 million adult females aged 18 or older who had a co-occurring SUD and MDE. Among these adult females, 38.0% had serious thoughts of suicide, 14.3% made a suicide plan, and 5.9% percent attempted suicide.
