### Strengths and Limitations

This study has several strengths and limitations. A key strength is the prepolicy and postpolicy comparison within a population-based, repeated cross-sectional design. The use of adolescent self-report is both a strength and potential limitation. Self-reports provide valuable information on mental health and experiences not captured in administrative or clinical records<sup>32</sup> but do not allow inference about clinical levels of disorder, and self-reported screen use is only weakly correlated with objectively measured use.<sup>33</sup> Similarly, mental health symptoms were assessed during the preceding week, which may have limited sensitivity to longer-term changes, although a short recall period may reduce retrospective recall demands and is commonly used in validated pediatric measures.<sup>34</sup>

Of the recruited pupil participants, 1223 (99.67%) provided data on mental wellbeing (primary outcome); 817 (99.63%) in the restrictive schools (intervention) and 406 (99.75%) in permissive schools (comparator) (Fig. 1). Table 2 shows the characteristics of schools and pupil participants. The 30 participating schools did not differ considerably from the 1341 schools in the sampling frame across all characteristics (Supplementary File). The sample of recruited schools with a permissive school phone policy (comparator group) included a greater proportion of selective and single sex schools, compared to the restrictive schools (intervention), however, this difference was proportionately reflected in the sampling frame (Supplementary File). Pupil characteristics were well balanced across comparator and intervention schools (Table 2).
