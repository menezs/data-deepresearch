# Study indicates that a four-day workweek requires trust and flexibility to generate results

#### Research shows that management models based on cooperation and autonomy favor productivity gains, well-being, and talent retention

An article by Fernando D. Domingos, professor at **São Paulo School of Administration (FGV EAESP)**, and Pedro Gomes, economics professor at Birkbeck, University of London, published in the *GV Executivo* magazine, analyzes the factors contributing to the success of the four-day workweek in organizations. 

The research begins with a central question: why do some companies manage to reduce working hours without losing productivity, while others struggle? According to the authors, the answer lies in the governance model adopted. Organizations operating on the basis of trust, cooperation, and shared goals tend to achieve more consistent results than those driven by rigid rules and strict control metrics.

The study was carried out in the context of the Brazilian pilot of the 4 Day Week Brazil initiative, inspired by international experiments. In 2024, nineteen companies from different sectors adopted the 100-80-100™ model, which entails 100% of salary, 80% of working hours, and maintenance of 100% productivity.

Over six months, the impacts of reduced hours were assessed in 252 professionals from small and medium-sized businesses in the fields of technology, consulting, design, communication, health, and financial services. Organizations located in cities such as São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, Belo Horizonte, and Curitiba participated.

Results indicate that the four-day workweek is most effective in flexible organizational environments where employee autonomy is valued and working relationships are supported by relational contracts based on mutual trust, commitment, and long-term alignment.

In addition to contributing to literature on new forms of work, the study reinforces that reducing hours can be a relevant strategy to tackle contemporary challenges, such as professional burnout and talent attraction and retention, provided that it is accompanied by changes in management culture and work organization.
