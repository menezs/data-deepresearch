## Semaglutide and Alzheimer’s: what were the EVOKE results?

The primary measure was the Clinical Dementia Rating–Sum of Boxes, or CDR-SB. It combines information about cognition and daily function; higher scores indicate greater impairment. The main comparison asked whether the score worsened less with semaglutide than with placebo after 104 weeks. Cummings 2026: primary results.

Average scores worsened in both groups. In EVOKE, the reported mean change was 2.3 points in each group. In EVOKE+, it was 2.2 with semaglutide and 2.1 with placebo. Neither trial showed a statistically significant difference on this endpoint. Their confidence intervals included zero, meaning the estimates were compatible with no difference between treatments. ## What do Alzheimer’s trials measure?

Alzheimer’s disease affects thinking and everyday activities. A trial can therefore track both cognitive abilities and how well someone manages daily life. Biomarkers, such as particular proteins measured in blood or the fluid surrounding the brain and spinal cord, describe biological processes. Their movement and a person’s clinical course are separate observations.

Semaglutide activates the receptor for glucagon-like peptide 1, abbreviated GLP-1, a hormone involved in metabolic signalling. Our peptide signalling introduction explains the underlying peptide terminology. The Alzheimer’s question is more specific: whether changing this pathway meaningfully alters disease progression in the people studied.

## Who took part in EVOKE and EVOKE+?

The two randomized, placebo-controlled trials enrolled 3,808 adults aged 55–85. Participants already had mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia due to Alzheimer’s, with amyloid pathology confirmed. Amyloid is a protein that accumulates abnormally in the disease. This was a treatment study in early symptomatic illness, rather than a prevention study in an unaffected population. Cummings 2026: trial design.

EVOKE randomized 1,855 people and EVOKE+ randomized 1,953. Both investigated oral semaglutide. EVOKE+ also allowed specified small-vessel changes in the brain, but only 54 participants, or 2.8%, had the qualifying pathology. Describing the entire second trial as a vascular-disease population would misrepresent its participants.

## What safety observations did the trials report?

Treatment-emergent adverse events were reported in 1,729 of 1,896 participants receiving semaglutide, or 91.2%, and 1,613 of 1,902 receiving placebo, or 84.8%. These are the safety-analysis populations, which differ from the randomized participant totals. Cummings 2026: safety population.

“Treatment-emergent” means an event started or worsened during the relevant observation period; it does not automatically mean the study medicine caused it. The percentages summarize any such event, rather than only serious events or only events leading to discontinuation.

Safety and efficacy answer complementary questions. In older adults with early symptomatic Alzheimer’s, the trial describes both the observed events and the absence of significant benefit on the main clinical measure. Its results offer evidence about that monitored setting, rather than an individual treatment plan.

### How should I compare prevention headlines with the EVOKE result?

First check whether participants already had Alzheimer’s symptoms. EVOKE assessed progression over 104 weeks in people with early symptomatic disease, whereas the record studies tracked first diagnoses over three years. These designs address different stages and questions, so an association with fewer new diagnoses does not contradict a negative progression trial. Cummings 2026: disease stage.

### Should I assume a million-person study means everyone received semaglutide?

No. The 2024 study had 1,094,761 eligible participants, but 17,104 were new semaglutide users; the others received comparison medicines. Keeping the exposed group separate from the full eligible population helps explain the headline. The analysis followed recorded diagnoses over three years and used statistical adjustment rather than random treatment assignment. Wang 2024: study population.

### How should I interpret the predicted dementia-risk reduction?

The SELECT analysis used a protein-based model in 2,970 older participants with paired samples over 104 weeks. Its five-year risk prediction was 26% lower relative to placebo. This describes a calculated prediction, not 26% fewer observed dementia cases; the longer prediction likewise does not represent 20 years of follow-up. Jiménez-Mausbach 2026: predicted risk.

The 2024 experiment involved 20 mice in four groups over four weeks and measured behaviours such as novel-object recognition. Such studies help investigate mechanisms and select questions for human trials. ## Why did some biomarkers change without clinical benefit?

Exploratory measurements in a subgroup examined cerebrospinal fluid, the fluid around the brain and spinal cord, at week 78. Several tau-related and inflammatory markers changed. Tau is a protein involved in Alzheimer’s pathology; measuring its different forms helps researchers investigate biological responses. Cummings 2026: biomarker analyses.

The blood findings were more selective: plasma p-tau181 and p-tau217 did not differ significantly between groups. A headline saying that all Alzheimer’s blood markers improved would therefore overstate the results. The exploratory comparisons also lacked adjustment for the many biomarker tests, increasing the chance of apparently positive findings.

Biological responses can help explain what a compound affects while clinical scores show whether the measured abilities changed. In this programme, some laboratory signals moved without a significant benefit on the main cognition-and-function measure. Both observations belong in the account, with the clinical endpoint carrying the main treatment conclusion.

## What did health records suggest about new diagnoses?

A 2024 study examined 1,094,761 eligible people with type 2 diabetes and no previous Alzheimer’s diagnosis. Of these, 17,104 started semaglutide. Researchers compared first recorded Alzheimer’s diagnoses over three years with those among users of other diabetes medicines, using a design called target-trial emulation to make the groups and follow-up more comparable. Wang 2024: diagnosis associations.

Semaglutide use was associated with a lower diagnosis rate. The hazard ratio was 0.33 against insulin and 0.59 against other GLP-1 medicines. A hazard ratio compares event occurrence over time; the different estimates also show how the comparator affects the result.

A related 2025 report broadened the outcome to Alzheimer’s disease and related dementias. Its eligible population was 1,710,995, including 64,267 semaglutide users, again followed for three years. It found lower associated rates against several comparators. The two reports draw on the same health-record environment and overlapping periods, so their populations should not be added together. Wang 2025: related dementias.

These are useful signals for future research. Prescribing choices, underlying health and differences in diagnosis can still influence an observational comparison after adjustment. Fewer first recorded diagnoses in diabetes care answers a different question from slowing established Alzheimer’s disease in a randomized trial.
