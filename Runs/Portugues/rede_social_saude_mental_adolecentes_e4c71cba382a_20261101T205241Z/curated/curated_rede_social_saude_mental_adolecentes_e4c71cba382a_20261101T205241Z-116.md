## The Burden Cannot Be Placed Entirely on Parents and Children

One of the Advisory’s most important conclusions is that families should not bear the entire responsibility for preventing harm. Parents cannot continuously monitor every recommendation, message, notification, design feature, or algorithmic decision affecting a child.

The Surgeon General calls on technology companies to conduct transparent and independent assessments of how their products affect children and adolescents. Companies are urged to prioritize health and safety during product development, use the highest safety and privacy settings by default for young users, enforce age restrictions, share relevant data with independent researchers, and create effective systems for addressing complaints.

The report also recommends that platform design avoid features whose primary purpose is maximizing time, attention, and engagement when those features present risks to children.

## A Safety-First Standard for Social Media Products

The Advisory compares social media safety to standards applied to toys, motor vehicles, and medications. In these industries, products intended for widespread use must satisfy baseline safety requirements, undergo testing, or incorporate protective features.

The Surgeon General recommends applying a similar safety-first principle to social media. Where mounting evidence identifies a risk of harm to children, safeguards should be implemented while rigorous and independent evaluations continue.

Under this approach, children would not be treated as the subjects of an uncontrolled experiment while companies retain exclusive access to the information needed to understand their products’ effects.

According to this principle, a basic threshold for safety must be met, and until safety is demonstrated with rigorous evidence and independent evaluation, protections are put in place to minimize the risk of harm from products, services, or goods. For example, the Consumer Product Safety Commission requires toy manufacturers to undergo third-party testing and be certified through a Children’s Product Certificate as compliant with the federal toy safety standard for toys intended for use by children. To reduce the risk of injury from motor 90 vehicle accidents, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration requires manufacturers to fit new motor vehicles with standard airbags and seat belts, among other safety features, and conduct crash tests to be compliant with the Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards. Medications must demonstrate safety 91 to the Food and Drug Administration before being made available and marketed for use. Given the mounting evidence for the risk of harm to some children and 92 adolescents from social media use, a safety-first approach should be applied in the context of social media products. 

To better safeguard the mental health and well-being of children and adolescents, policymakers, technology companies, researchers, families, and young people must all engage in a proactive and multifaceted approach. Through the recommendations below, we can provide more resources and tools to children and families, we can gain a better understanding of the full impact of social media, and we can maximize the benefits and minimize the harms of social media platforms to create safer, healthier online environments for children.
