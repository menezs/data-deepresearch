## Abstract

Human papillomavirus vaccination is central to cervical cancer elimination, yet population-level evidence from middle-income countries remains limited. We evaluated the impact of quadrivalent HPV vaccination in Brazil using data from two repeated population-based cross-sectional surveys conducted in 2016–2017 and 2020–2023 among sexually active individuals aged 16–25 years. Genital samples were tested for HPV genotyping and survey-weighted Poisson-regression models adjusted for sociodemographic and behavioral factors were used to compare prevalences between periods. Among 11,794 women and 4932 men, the prevalence of vaccine-type HPV declined from 15.64% (14.23–17.17) to 5.92% (5.23–6.70) among women and 13.81% (11.15–16.99) to 10.39% (8.35–12.85) among men. Adjusted analyses showed an 81% reduction among vaccinated women (PR0.19; 0.14–0.27) and a 33% reduction among unvaccinated women (PR0.67; 0.55–0.81), suggesting both direct and indirect effects. Reductions were significant only among women aged 16–23, the group with higher coverage. Among men, lower prevalence of vaccine-type HPV was observed only among vaccinated individuals (PR0.39; 0.18–0.86). The overall HPV prevalence increased modestly between the two surveys. No differences in prevalence reduction were observed according to the number of vaccine doses. These findings demonstrated the impact of HPV vaccination in Brazil and highlight the importance of maintaining high vaccination coverage and continued surveillance.

## Introduction

Vaccination against human papillomavirus (HPV) is one of the key prevention strategies to reduce HPV-associated cancers and achieve the World Health Organization (WHO) goals of cervical cancer elimination until 2030<sup>1,2</sup>. The quadrivalent HPV (4vHPV) vaccine, which protects against genotypes 6, 11, 16, and 18 (responsible for most genital warts and 80–90% of HPV-related cancers), has been incorporated into several national vaccination programs, pioneered by countries such as Australia, Mexico, and the United States as early as 2006<sup>2</sup>.

## Methods

### Study design/population

We used data from repeated cross-sectional surveys to compare genital HPV prevalence among sexually active 16- to 25-year-old men and women between pre- and post-vaccine eras in Brazil. The first study (POP-Brazil 1) was conducted between 2016 and 2017, in the 26 state capitals and the Federal District of Brazil, right after the introduction of the HPV vaccination program. The second study (POP-Brazil 2), was conducted between 2020 and 2023 with the same population target. Participants who had not initiated sexual activity, pregnant women, women who had given birth within the previous six months, women who had undergone a hysterectomy or trachelectomy, and women who had ever had grade 2 or higher cervical intraepithelial neoplasia were not included. Both phases of the POP-Brazil survey were conducted according to the original study protocol<sup>11</sup>, ensuring consistent methodological characteristics and data collection procedures that enabled data comparison across the two time periods.

Participants were recruited from public health units and responded to a structured questionnaire conducted by health professionals who received rigorous training and onsite certification. Vaccination status was assessed as a dichotomous variable (vaccinated/unvaccinated). Participants were classified as vaccinated against HPV if they self-reported vaccination or had documented vaccination history (verified at the time of participation or through medical records and vaccination registries), regardless of dose number and vaccine type. Time since vaccination (years) was calculated by subtracting self-reported age at vaccination from age at interview. Sociodemographic variables included age, self-reported skin color/ethnicity, and socioeconomic status, which was computed based on a score of self-reported number of household goods according with the Associação Brasileira de Empresas de Pesquisa (ABEP). We also explored alcohol consumption in the last year, partnership status, condom use during the last sexual intercourse, number of sexual partners in the last year, and the age of sexual debut. Because retesting for HIV is not recommended for individuals who are already confirmed to be HIV-positive, we first assessed HIV status. For those that are HIV negative, unsure, or never have been tested, an HIV test was offered, with the result being incorporated in the HIV status determination.
