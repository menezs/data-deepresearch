### Understanding the Tennessee ELVIS Act (AI Voice/Likeness)

The "Ensuring Likeness Voice and Image Security" (ELVIS) Act, though not formally assigned a bill number in the provided list, is a pivotal piece of legislation effective July 1, 2024. It significantly expands Tennessee's existing "right of publicity" laws to explicitly include protections for individual voices, images, and likenesses against unauthorized use, particularly in the context of generative AI technologies. This means that creating or distributing AI-generated content that mimics an individual's voice or appearance without proper consent can lead to substantial legal repercussions.

The penalties for violating the Tennessee ELVIS Act are severe, reaching up to $150,000 per violation. Enforcement can come through private rights of action, allowing affected individuals to sue, as well as through actions initiated by the Tennessee Attorney General. This dual enforcement mechanism underscores the critical need for proactive measures to prevent violations.

While the ELVIS Act does not explicitly mandate an AI Impact Assessment, the nature of the protections it affords—and the significant penalties for infringement—makes the AIIA an indispensable tool for proactive compliance and risk management. Companies deploying AI systems that could potentially generate or manipulate voice, image, or likeness data must assess their operations against these new requirements.

#### 3. Risk Identification and Assessment for Likeness/Voice Misuse

This is the core of the AIIA. Systematically identify potential risks related to unauthorized use of voice, image, or likeness. Consider:

- **Unauthorized Creation:** Could the AI system generate content that mimics a person's identity without explicit, verifiable consent?
- **Unauthorized Distribution:** Are there controls to prevent the distribution of such content, even if created by a third party?
- **Training Data Issues:** Was the training data used to develop the AI system sourced ethically and legally? Does it contain protected likenesses without proper licensing or consent?
- **Misattribution/Defamation:** Could the AI system be misused to create content that falsely attributes words or actions to an individual, potentially causing harm or reputational damage?
- **Consent Management:** Are your consent mechanisms robust enough to meet the ELVIS Act's implicit demand for clear permission regarding the use of voice, image, and likeness?
- **Third-Party AI Services:** If using external AI models, what are the contractual safeguards against ELVIS Act violations?
- **Emergent Risks:** Consider future capabilities or misuse cases not yet apparent.

Each identified risk should be assessed for its **likelihood** (how probable is it?) and **impact** (how severe would the consequences be?). A high likelihood and high impact risk, such as the unauthorized deepfake of a public figure for marketing, warrants immediate attention.

### Step-by-Step Guide to Conducting an ELVIS Act AI Impact Assessment

Here’s a practical, step-by-step guide to conducting an AIIA specifically tailored to address the requirements and spirit of the Tennessee ELVIS Act.

#### 1. Define the Scope and Purpose of the Assessment

Before diving in, clearly articulate which AI systems or use cases will be assessed. For ELVIS Act compliance, the focus should be on AI systems that:

- Generate synthetic media, including deepfakes of voices, images, or video.
- Are trained on datasets containing personally identifiable voice, image, or likeness data.
- Offer capabilities for voice cloning, face swapping, or digital avatar creation.
- Are used in marketing, entertainment, or public-facing applications where a person's identity might be imitated or recreated.

The purpose of the assessment is to identify potential ELVIS Act violations, assess associated risks, and develop mitigation strategies.

#### 2. Identify and Map All Relevant AI Systems

Create a comprehensive inventory of all AI systems within your organization that fall under the defined scope. This includes both internal systems under development and third-party AI tools or services your company utilizes. For each system, document:

- **System Name and Description:** What does it do?
- **Purpose and Use Cases:** How is it applied in your operations?
- **Data Inputs:** What types of data does it process, particularly voice, image, or likeness data?
- **Data Outputs:** What types of content does it generate?
- **Development Status:** In development, deployed, or retired?
- **Stakeholders:** Who are the developers, deployers, and users?

### Broader Implications and the Future Landscape of AI Regulation

This localized approach means that businesses operating nationally must contend with a patchwork of regulations. For instance, while ELVIS focuses on likeness, other states are addressing broader AI ethics. Texas, with its Responsible AI Governance Act (TRAIGA, HB 149), effective January 1, 2026, imposes penalties up to $200,000 per violation for certain AI uses. Furthermore, federal agencies like the FTC continue to enforce existing laws against deceptive AI practices under FTC Section 5, which can lead to penalties up to $50,000 per violation per day. The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0), though voluntary, provides a widely recognized standard for managing AI risks, which is often referenced by regulators as a best practice.

An AI Impact Assessment serves as a foundational element of any comprehensive AI risk management framework. It provides a structured approach to identifying, evaluating, and mitigating the potential risks associated with AI systems, particularly those related to bias, discrimination, privacy, and, in the context of the ELVIS Act, the unauthorized use of an individual's identity.

For AI systems that interact with or generate human likenesses or voices, an AIIA helps organizations:

- **Identify Exposure:** Pinpoint exactly where AI systems could infringe on an individual's right of publicity.

#### 4. Review Data Sourcing, Consent, and Intellectual Property

Given the ELVIS Act's focus, a deep dive into data practices is paramount.

- **Consent Verification:** For any AI system that uses or generates voice, image, or likeness data, confirm that all necessary consents have been obtained. This should be explicit, informed, and demonstrable. Generic terms of service may not suffice.
- **Licensing and Rights:** If using third-party datasets or copyrighted material, ensure proper licenses are in place that explicitly permit AI training and generation, especially concerning individual identities.
- **Data Provenance:** Document the origin of all relevant data. Can you trace back where voice or image data came from and confirm its legal acquisition?
- **Data Minimization:** Are you collecting and processing only the voice, image, or likeness data that is strictly necessary for the AI system's intended purpose?

This step links directly to broader data privacy concerns, overlapping with principles found in GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679, effective May 25, 2018, with penalties up to $20,000,000 per violation) and the Virginia CDPA (effective January 1, 2023, penalties up to $7,500 per violation) regarding personal data processing and profiling.

#### 5. Develop and Implement Mitigation Strategies

For each identified risk, develop concrete mitigation strategies. These can be categorized as:

- **Technical Controls:**  - Implementing watermarking or digital signatures for AI-generated media.
  - Developing robust detection mechanisms for deepfakes.
  - Access controls to AI generation tools.
  - Data anonymization or synthetic data generation techniques where applicable.
- **Organizational Controls:**  - Establishing clear internal policies and guidelines for the use of generative AI.
  - Mandatory training for employees on ELVIS Act compliance and responsible AI use.
  - Setting up approval workflows for any AI-generated content that could involve likenesses.
  - Creating a dedicated AI ethics or governance committee.
- **Legal/Contractual Controls:**  - Updating vendor contracts to include ELVIS Act compliance clauses.
  - Ensuring robust consent forms and agreements with individuals whose likenesses may be used.
  - Reviewing user agreements for AI products to clearly define permissible use.

An AI compliance tool can be invaluable here, helping to track the implementation status of these controls and map them back to identified risks.
