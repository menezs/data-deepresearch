# Latest Global Immunization Coverage Estimates Illustrate Gaps in Vaccine Access

According to recently released WHO-UNICEF Estimates of National Immunization Coverage (WUENIC), global vaccination coverage remained largely unchanged in 2025, leaving millions of children vulnerable to vaccine-preventable diseases. Nearly 116 million infants (90%) received at least one dose of a diphtheria, tetanus, and pertussis (DTP) vaccine, with 110 million (85%) completing the full three-dose series. An estimated 13.5 million zero-dose children did not receive their first dose of DTP vaccine in 2025, approximately 750,000 fewer children than 2024, but far too many children continue to miss out on life-saving vaccines.

Measles vaccine coverage has also stalled, with only 84% of children receiving their first dose of measles-containing vaccine (MCV1) and just 77% receiving their second dose. These numbers are far below the recommended 95% coverage with two doses of MCV required to prevent measles outbreaks. As a result, 57 countries reported large measles outbreaks in 2025. In 2025, MCV1 coverage was estimated to be very high (at or above 90%) in 96 countries, compared to 98 countries in 2024. Over the same time period, the number of countries with very low estimated MCV1 coverage (<60%) decreased slightly from 15 to 14.


Although national introduction of human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccination continues to ramp up, only one-third of 9–14-year-old girls globally received at least one dose of HPV vaccine in 2025. However, 15 countries introduced HPV vaccines into their nationwide immunization programs in 2025, including China, greatly increasing access. Still, 35% of eligible children live in countries that have not yet introduced HPV vaccines, which are a critical part of cancer prevention strategies. In spite of these 15 introductions, the number of girls reached with HPV vaccines did not increase as significantly as expected due to fewer girls reached in countries that had previously introduced HPV vaccines. As advocates continue to push for new introductions, efforts are still needed to maintain and strengthen existing HPV vaccination programs.

Other notable trends in the latest WUENIC data include regional differences in performance, with the Americas and South-East Asia regions fully recovered from pandemic-related disruptions and the Western Pacific region furthest below its 2019 baseline. Importantly, more than half of all zero-dose children were estimated to live in fragile or conflict-affected countries, highlighting the need for innovative strategies to maintain access to these vulnerable groups. Lastly, access does not always translate to vaccination, with coverage slipping among children in many middle- and high-income countries due to wavering political commitment and increasing vaccine hesitancy.

Moving forward, members of the global health community will need to work together to advocate for expanded access to life-saving vaccines while developing innovative strategies to reach every child.

– William Moss


Though many countries have successfully bounced back from post-pandemic coverage drops, we are still largely off track from global immunization targets. “Moving forward, members of the global health community will need to work together to advocate for expanded access to life-saving vaccines while developing innovative strategies to reach every child,” said IVAC Executive Director William Moss. “These latest estimates demonstrate that our immunization programs must remain strong and resilient in the face of decreased funding for global public health."
